\section{Proof of the harmonic continuum formula}

The Euclidean action of a harmonic oscillator $V=\tfrac12 m\omega^2 q^2$ is diagonal in Matsubara modes
\begin{equation}
  q(\tau)=\frac{1}{\beta\hbar}\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\tilde q_n e^{i\omega_n\tau},
  \qquad
  \omega_n=\frac{2\pi n}{\beta\hbar}.
\end{equation}
Each mode is Gaussian with $\avg{|\tilde q_n|^2}=\kB T\,\beta\hbar/[m(\omega_n^2+\omega^2)]$. The equal-time correlator is
\begin{equation}
  G(0)
  =
  \frac{\kB T}{m}\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\omega_n^2+\omega^2}
  =
  \frac{\hbar}{2m\omega}\coth\Bigl(\frac{\beta\hbar\omega}{2}\Bigr),
\end{equation}
where the sum is the standard cotangent partial-fraction identity. The $n=0$ sector is the centroid, with variance $\kB T/(m\omega^2)$, which is the classical Boltzmann result for a harmonic well.\cite{cao1994} Subtracting gives
\begin{equation}
  \avg{\chip}=G(0)-\avg{\xip^2},
\end{equation}
which is Eq.~(8) of the main text. Non-negativity follows from $\coth x\ge 1/x$.

Low $T$: $\coth(\beta\hbar\omega/2)\to 1$ and the centroid term vanishes, so $\avg{\chip}\to\hbar/(2m\omega)$. High $T$: $\coth x=1/x+x/3+O(x^3)$ yields $\avg{\chip}\to\hbar^2\beta/(12m)+O(\beta^3)$.

\section{Exact finite-bead formula}

The $P$-bead ring polymer is a cyclic Gaussian with eigenvalues
\begin{equation}
  \lambda_k
  =
  \frac{4mP}{\hbar^2\beta^2}\sin^2(\pi k/P)
  +\frac{m\omega^2}{P},
  \qquad
  k=0,\ldots,P-1.
\end{equation}
The $k=0$ eigenvalue is $m\omega^2/P$ and produces the centroid variance $\kB T/(m\omega^2)$. The primitive bead variance minus the centroid variance is
\begin{equation}
  \avg{\chip}_P
  =
  \frac{1}{P}\sum_{k=1}^{P-1}\frac{1}{\beta\lambda_k}.
\end{equation}
As $P\to\infty$ the sum becomes the Matsubara sum of the previous section.

\section{Isotope ordering (harmonic)}

With $k=m\omega^2$ fixed, $\omega(m)=\sqrt{k/m}$ and
\begin{equation}
  g(m)
  =
  \frac{\hbar}{2\sqrt{km}}\coth\Bigl(\frac{\beta\hbar\sqrt{k/m}}{2}\Bigr)
  -\frac{\kB T}{k}.
\end{equation}
The second term is mass-independent. The first is $( \kB T/k)\,u\coth u$ with $u=\beta\hbar\sqrt{k/m}/2$. Since $u\coth u$ is strictly increasing for $u>0$ and $u$ is strictly decreasing in $m$, $g(m)$ is strictly decreasing, so $g(m_H)>g(m_D)$. The $T\to 0$ and $T\to\infty$ ratios follow by substituting the limits of the previous section. These statements are harmonic; they are not theorems for a general anharmonic surface.

\section{Eckart centroid QTST}

On the symmetric Eckart barrier the centroid QTST rate is
\[
  k_{\mathrm{cTST}}=\sqrt{k_{\mathrm{B}}T/(2\pi m)}\,e^{-\beta F(0)},
\]
with $F$ the WHAM potential of mean force zeroed on the $|\bar\xi_p|>1.6\,\text{\AA}$ tails. With a class-independent transmission coefficient $\kappa=1$, the QRR integral of the main text is identically $k_{\mathrm{cTST}}$. Residual tail scatter in $F$ is of order $0.5\,\mathrm{kJ\,mol}^{-1}$. Deuterium at $V_0=12\,k_{\mathrm{B}}T$ is excluded from the rate comparison: the dividing-surface window drifted and produced $F_D(0)<F_H(0)$, which is unphysical. Path-class fractions $f_{\mathrm{deloc}}$ at that point remain consistent with the other deuterium barriers and are retained in Fig.~4(c) of the main text.

The isolated asymmetric double well is bound. It has no irreversible thermal rate, so DVR ``rates'' on that potential are not a benchmark.
